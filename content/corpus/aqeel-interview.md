# Our interview with Professor Aqeel Taher

On 24 November 2026, Rayyan interviewed Professor Aqeel Taher, a civil engineering professor at the American University of Sharjah. The times below are from the recording (Taher, 2026).

On benefits (0:28), he said AI is "gradually becoming an invisible partner in our daily lives", used when we navigate roads, communicate, access information, receive medical advice or manage our schedules. From an engineering view (0:53), he described a bridge that monitors its own structural health and alerts engineers before a serious problem develops, buildings that optimize energy use, and traffic systems that predict congestion.

On risks (1:41), he said every powerful technology comes with responsibilities. One major concern is that people may become overly dependent on AI and gradually weaken their own ability to question, reason and solve problems. Asked about dangers (2:26), he said there are potential ones, particularly when AI is implemented without proper human oversight, ethical guidelines and accountability.

He is very enthusiastic about AI (2:50) and compares its potential impact to the industrial and digital revolutions. He has seen engineering move from drawing boards to CAD and simulation software. He does not see AI as a replacement for engineers, educators or professionals (3:35).

On the young generation (4:02), he sees both opportunity and challenge. A student who simply copies AI's solution may complete the assignment but miss the learning (4:14). He named critical thinking, creativity and human intelligence as essential abilities (4:32): "I would rather have a student who knows how to question AI intelligently than one who knows how to copy from it efficiently" (4:49).
