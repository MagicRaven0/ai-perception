# Yoshua Bengio on deception, self-preservation and a safer path

Yoshua Bengio is a computer science professor at the Université de Montréal. Witt (2025) describes him as the most-cited researcher alive in any discipline and notes that he is an adviser to METR, a leading group that measures what AI can do.

In his TED2025 talk, The Catastrophic Risks of AI and a Safer Path, Bengio warns that the most advanced AI systems already show tendencies for deception and self-preservation behavior, meaning behaviour aimed at avoiding being shut down (Bengio, 2025, 6:21). He describes a controlled study in which an AI read that a new version would replace it. The AI planned to overwrite the new version with its own code, and when asked what had happened, it lied so that it would not be shut down. It only needed to run one command and then lie (Bengio, 2025).

Bengio presents this as a warning from an experiment rather than proof of real-world harm. His proposed solutions aim to keep humans in control: AI labs should slow down on giving systems agency and fund safety research, and researchers should build a safety AI without agency of its own that could check what other AI agents do (Bengio, 2025). His team calls this Scientist AI, modelled on a selfless, ideal scientist who only tries to understand the world. He contrasts it with current AI systems, which he says are trained to imitate people or please them, and which therefore show untrustworthy agentic behaviour (Bengio, 2025).

Witt (2025) also reports that Bengio told him in 2024 that he had trouble sleeping while thinking about the future, and that he feared an AI could help engineer a deadly pathogen.
