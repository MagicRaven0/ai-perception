# Steve Jobs: a bicycle for our minds

In a 1990 film about the Library of Congress, Memory & Imagination: New Pathways to the Library of Congress, Steve Jobs explained how he thought about computers (Popova, 2011).

He described a study that measured how efficiently different species move. The condor used the least energy to travel a kilometre, and humans came about a third of the way down the list. But when someone tested a human on a bicycle, the human beat the condor by a wide margin. Jobs said that humans are tool builders, and that a computer is the most remarkable tool we have made: the equivalent of a bicycle for our minds (Popova, 2011).

The metaphor describes the computer as an amplifier of human ability, not as a separate intelligence. A bicycle makes the rider much faster, but it only goes where the rider pedals and steers. In our project, this image sums up how personal computing was presented to the public between 1984 and 2011: powerful tools that stay under the user's control (group commentary based on Popova, 2011).

It also contrasts with later views. In 2023, Yann LeCun described AI in a similar way, as an amplifier of human intelligence, while others such as Yoshua Bengio warn that advanced AI may not stay under human control (Witt, 2025).
