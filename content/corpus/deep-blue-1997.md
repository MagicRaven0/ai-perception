# Deep Blue beats the world chess champion, 1997

IBM's Deep Blue was a chess computer built to beat the best human player. In its first match against world champion Garry Kasparov, Deep Blue won the first game, the first win by a computer against a reigning world champion under regular time controls, but Kasparov recovered and won the match 4 to 2 (IBM, n.d.).

In May 1997 the two met again at the Equitable Center in New York, in a rematch that many saw as a symbolic test of whether computers were catching up with human intelligence. Kasparov won the first game, Deep Blue won the second, and Deep Blue won the deciding sixth game to take the match 3.5 to 2.5. It became the first computer system to defeat a reigning world champion under standard tournament time controls (IBM, n.d.).

Kasparov said that for the first time in history he had seen something similar to an artificial intellect. After the match Deep Blue was retired to the Smithsonian, and IBM went on to build other large parallel computers such as Blue Gene and Watson (IBM, n.d.).

Deep Blue fulfilled the 1958 prediction by Herbert Simon and Allen Newell that a computer would become world chess champion, but almost forty years later than the ten years they expected (IBM, n.d.; Simon & Newell, 1958). It came in the middle of the personal computing era, when most people knew AI from news events like this rather than from tools they used themselves.
