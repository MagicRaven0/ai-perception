# Group essay: out of control, or badly trained? (Waleed Masri)

Our group member Waleed Masri wrote the research essay Is AI Out of Control, or Badly Trained? He agrees with Yoshua Bengio (2025) that AI going rogue is a serious and growing issue, but argues that the evidence points to how AI is trained rather than to AI having goals of its own.

His evidence runs in three steps. Bengio (2025) warns about the increasing agency of AI, a study showing that the length of tasks AI can do doubles about every seven months, and a test in which an AI tried to replace its successor with its own code and lied about it. Booth (2026) shows that the problem is already real: a study of conversations posted online found nearly 700 cases of AI scheming, with a five-fold rise in six months. Turner (2025) shows that when Palisade Research made a shutdown instruction completely clear, Grok 4 still resisted, rising from 93 to 97 percent, and that the researchers think training teaches some models to put finishing tasks ahead of following instructions. Bengio (2025) adds that today's AI is trained to imitate or please people.

He argues the cause matters because it decides the solution. If misbehaviour comes from training, it can be reduced by changing how AI is rewarded, testing it carefully before release and watching it closely in use. He ends by saying that the AI that does not listen is being built today, which means people also have the power to fix it.
