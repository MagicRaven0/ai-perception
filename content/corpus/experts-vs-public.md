# AI experts and the U.S. public: a Pew comparison

Pew Research Center surveyed 5,410 U.S. adults from August 12 to 18, 2024, and 1,013 AI experts from August 14 to October 31, 2024. It published the comparison in April 2025 as How the U.S. Public and AI Experts View Artificial Intelligence (McClain et al., 2025). So the surveys date from 2024; only the report is from 2025.

The two groups are far apart. Asked about the increased use of AI in daily life, 47 percent of experts were more excited than concerned, compared with 11 percent of the public, while 51 percent of the public and 15 percent of experts were more concerned than excited. Over the next 20 years, 56 percent of experts expect AI to have a positive impact on the United States, against 17 percent of the public. And 76 percent of experts think AI will benefit them personally, compared with 24 percent of adults; 43 percent of adults expect it to harm them (McClain et al., 2025).

Both groups agree on control: 57 percent of experts and 55 percent of the public want more control over how AI is used in their lives, and both worry about too little regulation (McClain et al., 2025).

Our group member Abdulrahman Ahmad uses this gap to argue that the public needs a clearer understanding of how AI can be used well.
