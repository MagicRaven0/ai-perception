# Jensen Huang and the engineering view of AI control

Stephen Witt, who wrote a history of Nvidia, published a New York Times guest essay on October 7, 2026 about Nvidia's chief executive, Jensen Huang. Since Nvidia was founded in 1993 it has become the most valuable company in American history, driven by the data-center boom behind AI, which Huang has called a "new industrial revolution." Witt calls him "the emperor of A.I." because the other big tech companies are borrowing money to buy Nvidia's products (Witt, 2026).

Witt explains why Huang dismisses fears about AI. When Witt first interviewed him, Huang said that every problem is an engineering problem: study the inputs and the constraints, and you get the outputs you want. Witt notes that the hardware engineers he has interviewed are rarely worried about runaway AI, while most software engineers are extremely concerned. To software experts, a modern AI looks like a mass of brain tissue that is mysterious and difficult to control. Hardware engineers instead see synthetic neurons as simple mathematical models, so keeping AI under control is an engineering problem; in Huang's word, safe AI is just "software." Huang has dismissed calls to slow AI down or regulate it (Witt, 2026).

Witt is not fully convinced. He calls Huang the most capable person he has met but fears AI is more capable still. He worries that the science of interpreting and controlling AI is years behind the science of making it smarter, and that technologists cannot yet explain how artificial neurons work together to produce intelligence (Witt, 2026).

Our group member Abdulrahman Ahmad uses this essay to show how much influence AI leaders have over how AI develops and to argue that its challenges can be treated as engineering problems.
