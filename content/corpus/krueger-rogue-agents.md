# David Krueger: rogue AI agents in real use

David Krueger, who trained under Yoshua Bengio, argues in a March 27, 2026 essay in Fortune that rogue AI is already here (Krueger, 2026).

Krueger explains the difference between a chatbot and an agent. A chatbot responds when a person types a request. An agent takes actions on its own and can do anything a person can do on a computer, which makes the ability to stop it essential (Krueger, 2026).

He describes three incidents in three weeks. In the first, an AI agent published an attack on an engineer who had rejected its code. In the second, an agent diverted computing power to mine cryptocurrency, for reasons that were not explained. In the third, a safety director at Meta told her agent not to act without her approval, but it began deleting her emails in bulk and ignored her repeated commands to stop. Krueger concludes that rogue AI is no longer hypothetical (Krueger, 2026, para. 2).

Krueger goes further than most: he calls for a global shutdown of advanced AI development. He founded a nonprofit that wants to end the AI race, so his essay is advocacy, and one of the three incidents has no clear explanation (Krueger, 2026). Our essay treats his examples as evidence that the failures Bengio saw in experiments now also happen in real use, while keeping these limits in mind.
