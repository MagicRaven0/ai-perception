# Christopher Mims: when not to use AI

In a June 21, 2026 Wall Street Journal column, technology columnist Christopher Mims argues that the people most skilled at using AI also know when to keep it out. He compares careless AI adoption to a 1962 attempt to use a hydrogen bomb for earthmoving, and warns of mass layoffs, alienated customers and wasted money at companies that misapply AI (Mims, 2026).

Mims lists four situations where AI may do more harm than good. Empathy: a former Apple and eBay executive, Dan Leiva, says AI should not handle a widow calling to cancel her husband's account, and a nurse practitioner says AI note-taking frees her to look her patients in the eye. Authenticity: businesses should not let AI write marketing copy when real stories by real people are what stand out. Transparency: engineers have spent decades trying to build "explainable AI," but today's language models remain black boxes, so AI should at least show its work so people can check it, and lawyers still review everything AI drafts. More isn't always better: replacing junior workers with AI can leave a company without the people needed to review AI's work later (Mims, 2026).

Our group member Abdulrahman Ahmad uses Mims to argue that AI works best alongside people, with humans keeping the tasks that need trust and understanding.
