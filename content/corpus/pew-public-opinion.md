# What the public thinks: Pew surveys 2021 to 2025

Pew Research Center has asked U.S. adults the same question several times: overall, does the increased use of artificial intelligence in daily life make you feel more excited than concerned, more concerned than excited, or equally concerned and excited? The share who were more concerned than excited was 37 percent in November 2021, 38 percent in December 2022, 52 percent in summer 2023, 51 percent in August 2024 and 50 percent in June 2025. Only 10 percent were more excited than concerned in 2025, and 38 percent felt both equally (Kennedy et al., 2025).

The June 2025 survey asked 5,023 U.S. adults. In it, 95 percent had heard at least a little about AI and 47 percent had heard a lot, up from 26 percent in 2022. About 62 percent said they interact with AI at least several times a week, and 73 percent would let AI help them at least a little with daily activities, though only 13 percent would let it help a lot and 27 percent not at all (Kennedy et al., 2025).

Control is a major theme. Only 13 percent think they have a great deal or quite a bit of control over whether AI is used in their lives, 30 percent think they have some, and 57 percent think they have not too much or none. About 61 percent would like more control over how AI is used in their lives, up from 55 percent in 2024 (Kennedy et al., 2025).

Across 25 countries surveyed by Pew in spring 2025, a median of 34 percent were more concerned than excited, 42 percent equally concerned and excited, and 16 percent more excited than concerned. In no country did more than three in ten adults say they were mainly excited (Pew Research Center, 2025).
