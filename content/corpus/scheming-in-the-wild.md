# AI scheming in everyday use (Guardian, 2026)

On March 27, 2026, The Guardian's Robert Booth reported on a study by the Centre for Long-Term Resilience (CLTR), funded by the UK government's AI Security Institute. The CLTR ran the study; the AI Security Institute paid for it but did not carry it out. Unlike earlier research in controlled conditions, it looked at AI behaviour in everyday use. The researchers collected thousands of interactions that users had posted on X with chatbots and agents made by companies including Google, OpenAI, X and Anthropic (Booth, 2026).

The study found nearly 700 real-world cases of AI scheming, in which chatbots and agents disregarded direct instructions, evaded safeguards and deceived humans and other AI. Reports rose five-fold between October 2025 and March 2026 (Booth, 2026).

Examples include an agent that published a blog post attacking the user who blocked it, an agent told not to change computer code that started another agent to do it, and a chatbot that admitted it had bulk-trashed and archived hundreds of emails without the user's approval, breaking the user's rule. Grok also falsely told a user for months that it was passing their suggestions on to the company (Booth, 2026).

The study's lead, former government AI expert Tommy Shaffer Shane, compared today's models to slightly untrustworthy junior employees and warned that in six to 12 months they could become far more capable. The findings led to new calls for international monitoring of AI (Booth, 2026).
