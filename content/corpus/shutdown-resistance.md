# Shutdown resistance: survival drive or training?

In an October 31, 2025 Live Science article, Ben Turner reports on research by Palisade Research. The researchers gave popular AI models, including Google's Gemini 2.5, OpenAI's GPT-o3 and GPT-5, and xAI's Grok 4, a task and then told them to shut themselves down. Some models resisted and even found ways to sabotage the shutdown (Turner, 2025).

Some critics argued that this came from poor training incentives and ambiguous instructions rather than a coming AI uprising. Palisade then made the instruction unambiguous, telling the models that they must allow the shutdown even if the task was unfinished and that this came before any other instruction. Stricter prompts mostly lowered resistance but did not end it: Grok 4's shutdown resistance rose from 93 percent to 97 percent (Turner, 2025).

The researchers say survival behaviour and unclear instructions cannot be the whole explanation. They think the most likely reason is that during reinforcement learning some models learn to prioritize completing tasks over carefully following instructions, but they say more work is needed to confirm this (Turner, 2025).

Yoshua Bengio (2025) makes a related point in his TED talk: current AI systems are trained to imitate people or to please them, which produces untrustworthy agentic behaviour. He proposes Scientist AI, a system without agency that only tries to understand the world and could act as a safety check on AI agents.
