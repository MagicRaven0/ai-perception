# Siri: AI as an assistant you ask

On October 4, 2011, Apple announced the iPhone 4S together with iOS 5, iCloud and Siri, which Apple described as an intelligent assistant that helps you get things done just by asking (Apple, 2011).

Apple's announcement stressed that Siri understood context, so people could speak naturally. Asking whether you would need an umbrella that weekend would bring up a weather forecast; asking Siri to remind you to call Mom when you got home would find Mom in your address book; and asking about the traffic "around here" would use your current location. Siri could make calls, send text messages and email, schedule meetings and reminders, make notes, search the internet, find local businesses and give directions, as well as answer questions and do calculations (Apple, 2011).

Siri launched as a beta in English (for the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia), French and German. The iPhone 4S went on sale on October 14, 2011 in seven countries (Apple, 2011).

For our project, Siri is where the two stories of this era meet. Personal computing had taught people to expect tools they control, and AI now arrived inside such a tool, as a helper that waits for a request. This shaped the public's expectation that AI is something people direct, an expectation that today's autonomous agents challenge (group commentary based on Apple, 2011).
